## Executive summary: the findings that matter most

1. **These are not two unrelated channels.** The niche is the same: crude-humour, animal-mascot self-improvement for young men. The mascot is near-identical: a hand-drawn brown gorilla with a beige face and dot eyes. B's early titles copied A's format ("…Explained By Monke" vs "…Explained By Ape"). B's Dec-2025 testosterone video re-uses whole passages of A's Jul-2025 testosterone script. The cold open, five-section order, sentence structure, and closing all match ([evidence](09-comparative-analysis.md#1-the-relationship-between-the-channels)). Public data can't tell us whether this is a shared team, a licensed script, or one channel adapting the other.
2. **They have since diverged into different businesses.** **A** is a *fitness/physique explainer* that earns from sponsor reads (Cal AI) and merch. **B** has become a *life-direction/discipline* channel that funnels viewers into its own subscription habit app ("Monke – Habit Game", iOS), a Skool community, Discord, and Substack.
3. **Both channels are hit-driven.** A's top two videos are ~14× its median. B has 7 videos over 1M against a 58K median. 21 of B's 50 long-form videos with public view counts are under 30K. Totals: A ≈ 7.8M long-form views, B ≈ 15.9M.
4. **The trajectories run opposite ways.** A's 2025 uploads have a median of 338K, H1-2026 56K, and H2-2026 90K (decline, partial recovery). B's 2025 uploads have a median of 14K, H1-2026 35.5K, and H2-2026 194K (steep growth). B's newest breakout, "How to become dangerously self-educated." (Oct 13, 2026), reached **1.42M views in 26 days (~54.6K/day)**, the fastest of any video in either library.
5. **The winning topics are taboo-adjacent male-body anxieties**: testosterone, porn/"gooning", penis size ("banana"), skin, sleep. On B, broad "fix your life" framing also wins. The losing topics are repetitive fat-loss variants (A) and generic productivity/procrastination (B: 18 videos, median 25K).
6. **What separates hits from misses, as far as the data shows:**
   - **(a)** The hits open with a story or skit, not a definition.
   - **(b)** Narration on the three biggest hits with transcripts runs at **127–132 words per minute**, versus a 165–173 channel median.
   - **(c)** The hit thumbnails carry one bold visual pun or a "loser vs winner" body contrast.
   - **(d)** A's four weakest 2026 videos each carry a near-verbatim ~50–65 s Cal AI read (14–22% of runtime). One sponsored video, "10x Testosterone", still reached 369K, so the read alone isn't fatal; repeating the same ad over the same advice is the likelier problem.
   - **(e)** Topic repetition hurts. One of A's own weak videos admits "for the past three to four videos, it has been the same advice."
7. **The format has become a genre.** YouTube autocomplete for "explained by monkey" now returns *economy, crypto, tariffs, capitalism, BF6, creatine, dopamine, AI bubble*. "Monkey explains" suggests *inflation, credit cards, taxes, stocks, tariffs, SpaceX*. A mascot-explainer format can now travel across niches, and the monkey version is increasingly crowded.
8. **Gaps worth noting:** neither channel addresses women. Both make factual claims of uneven accuracy (e.g. "10x your testosterone"; the 1998 "ego depletion" study told as settled fact, though it has well-known replication failures). Shorts are weak for both (B's Shorts median: 5.7K). Searches show demand that neither channel's catalogue covers: "creatine explained by ape", "sleepmaxxing", "cortisol face", "how to become someone everyone loves talking to".

## Methodology

| Source | What was collected | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Channel pages (uploads + Shorts tabs) | Titles, order, rounded view counts, durations, subscriber counts, descriptions | **92 / 92 videos** |
| Full video metadata | Exact upload date, exact views, likes, comment counts, description, tags, auto-captions, storyboard frames | **30 / 92 videos** (10 A, 20 B), including 4 of the 9 videos over 1M |
| A's channel RSS feed | Exact date, exact views and likes for A's 15 most recent uploads | 15 A videos |
| Auto-captions (English) | Full timestamped transcripts → WPM, hooks, structure, sponsor timing, script reuse | 30 videos |
| YouTube storyboards (sb0: 160×90 px frames sampled every ~1, 2 or 5 s) | Frame-by-frame visual timeline, colour sampling, visual-change frequency | 30 videos |
| Thumbnails (1280×720) | Inspected individually, colour-sampled, outline thickness measured | **92 / 92** |
| Avatars and banners | Brand-identity analysis, colour sampling | Both channels |
| YouTube autocomplete (Google suggest endpoint, `ds=yt`) | ~400 suggestions across 40 seed terms | Snapshot 2026-11-08 |
| One captured watch page | YouTube's auto-generated "key moments" for A's #2 video, plus the "Up next" recommendation list (competitor adjacency) | 1 page |
| Web search | Trend coverage (looksmaxxing, "maxxing" word of the year, winter arc), B's app listing, monkey-finance channels | Cited inline |

### Evidence labels used throughout
- **[Verified]**: read directly from YouTube metadata or a cited source.
- **[Measured]**: computed by me from the collected data (WPM, colour sampling, frame-change rates).
- **[Estimate]**: inferred with stated uncertainty (interpolated dates, timing from sampled frames).
- **[Interpretation]**: my strategic reading of the evidence.

## Research limitations (read before relying on any number)

1. **YouTube blocked the research environment partway through.** After the first metadata pass, YouTube returned *HTTP 429* and then *"Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"* for this machine's IP. I stopped instead of trying to get around the check. Consequences:
   - **Exact upload dates, likes and comment counts exist for only 45 of 92 videos.** The other dates are **interpolated** from upload order. Each one carries its bracket in the [inventory](appendix-inventory.md).
   - **No comment text was collected.** The [audience research](06-audience-research.md) therefore rests on content, titles, community links, engagement ratios and autocomplete, not on comment-section reading. This is the biggest gap in the report.
   - **Google Trends** also returned HTTP 429. No Trends curves are claimed. Trend evidence comes from autocomplete plus published reporting.
2. **I could not watch the videos with sound.** The video and audio streams sit on hosts this environment cannot reach. Visual analysis uses YouTube's storyboard frames: real frames, but small (160×90) and sampled every 1, 2 or 5 seconds, so very fast cuts and in-between motion are invisible. Audio analysis (voice timbre, music, SFX, mix) **could not be done directly**. Section 6 of each channel report states only what transcripts reveal (e.g. `[music]`, `[screaming]` tags, multi-voice skits) and marks everything else unknown.
3. **No private analytics.** Retention, CTR, impressions, traffic sources and demographics are not public. None are claimed. Proxies are labelled as proxies.
4. **Rounded counts.** For 47 videos, views are YouTube's rounded display values ("1.5M").
5. **One B video ("Give me 5 minutes, I'll DESTROY your p*rn addiction forever", position 4) shows no public view count.** It may have been a premiere, or its count is hidden. It is excluded from the statistics.

## Quality control (Section 18 checklist)

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Did I actually inspect both channels? | **Yes.** Channel pages, all 92 uploads, both avatars and banners |
| Did I keep the identities separate? | **Yes.** Separate reports (01, 02). The relationship evidence is isolated in 09 §1 |
| Enough videos to establish patterns? | **Yes for packaging and performance** (92/92). **Partly for content:** 30 transcripts and frame strips (10 A, 20 B) |
| Successful and unsuccessful uploads both examined? | **Yes.** Autopsies cover top, mid and bottom videos on each channel |
| Looked beyond view counts? | **Yes.** Views/day, age-matched multiples, like and comment rates, pillar and format medians |
| Animation analysed beyond generic labels? | **Yes, within the limits of 160×90 frames.** Character construction, measured outline thickness, sampled HEX, hold durations. Easing and squash-and-stretch are **not observable** |
| Timing and pacing? | **Yes.** Word-timed hooks, WPM, hold lengths, timestamped timelines |
| Thumbnails individually? | **Yes.** All 22 of A's; 24 of B's 50 in the table, all 50 inspected |
| Audience comments? | **No.** Blocked (see limitations). Substituted with engagement ratios, scripts, community links and autocomplete |
| Current trend evidence? | **Partly.** Autocomplete and 2026 reporting yes; Google Trends blocked |
| Public vs private analytics kept apart? | **Yes.** No retention, CTR or demographic numbers claimed |
| Evidence cited? | **Yes.** Video links with timestamps; source links for external claims |
| Uncertainties identified? | **Yes.** Labels [Verified] / [Measured] / [Estimate] / [Interpretation]; estimated dates bracketed |
| Unsupported assumptions avoided? | Where a claim couldn't be checked (voice type, software used, ownership of the channels) it is marked unknown |
| Opportunities rather than copying? | **Yes.** 10 explicitly rejects re-using the gorilla archetype and the saturated topics |

**Not investigated, and why:** comment text and Google Trends (blocked by rate limits and bot checks); audio (stream hosts unreachable); full-resolution motion (storyboards only); the remaining 62 videos' scripts and frames (blocked after the first pass).
