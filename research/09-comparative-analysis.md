### 1.2 Other shared signals [Verified]
- **Title template:** B's earliest long-form titles copy A's suffix: "How To Focus **Explained By Monke**" (2025-10-24), "How To Grow Taller **Explained By Monke**" (before 2025-10-21). A has used "…Explained By Ape" since before Jul 2025.

- **Timing:** B's oldest dated upload is a Short from 2025-09-28. A's two biggest hits came out 2025-07-24 and 2025-10-03.
- **A's only Short** is titled "How to get abs explained by **monkey**".

### 1.3 What can't be determined
Public data can't distinguish between these explanations:
- (a) one creator or studio running two brands;
- (b) a shared writer or animator;
- (c) a licensed or purchased script;
- (d) one channel adapting the other.

**This report makes no claim about ownership or wrongdoing.** It records the overlap because it changes the strategic reading: the two "case studies" are best treated as **one format that has split into two business models**.

## 3. The ten questions

**1. What does A do particularly well?**
- **Thumbnail legibility.** The white field, one massive brown silhouette and one pun make the best-reading thumbnails in the set.
- **Search-native packaging.** The exact query plus a brand suffix now shows up in autocomplete ("…explained by ape").
- **Innuendo as a share engine.** Banana and coconut make taboo topics clickable and shareable.
- **Cheap, repeatable production.**

**2. What does B do particularly well?**
- **Story-led cold opens** that cast the viewer as Monkey in a specific embarrassing moment.
- **Identity-level, contrarian titles** that travel on recommendations.
- **A wider topic range** beyond the gym.
- **Weekly cadence.**
- **An owned product funnel** (app plus community), with the plug written in as part of the advice ("track this habit").
- **A voice that evolved** from gimmick caveman-speak into a real narrator.

**3. Biggest differences.**
- Business model: sponsor and merch (A) vs owned app and community (B).
- Scope: body (A) vs life (B).
- Structure: checklist (A) vs story arc (B).
- Tone: pure comedy (A) vs comedy plus sincerity (B).
- Packaging: search titles (A) vs browse titles (B).
- Cadence: irregular (A) vs weekly (B).

**4. What they share.**
- The same mascot archetype, flat hand-drawn style, high-key backgrounds and thick black outline.
- Monkey-voice narration.
- Testosterone and porn as top topics.
- Short-to-mid runtimes.
- A signature sign-off.
- Hit-driven view distributions.
- The same audience pool.
- At least one shared script.

**5. Unique elements.**
- **A:** handwritten section titles with banana sparkles; real-human contrast characters; the banana / coconut pun system; the mirror-catchphrase ending; tier-list and molecule thumbnails.
- **B:** cream colour field; loser / winner T-pose pair; whiteboard-card device; app UI cards; "Stay Monke" armchair end card; British-flavoured crude humour; named theories and parables.

**6. Principles worth learning from both** (as principles, not assets):
- (a) Open on a specific, recognisable moment of shame or frustration. Delay the promise until the story has earned it.
- (b) One literal visual per sentence, held ~2–3 s.
- (c) A single, ownable colour field plus one mascot silhouette for thumbnail recognition.
- (d) Euphemism as a device that lets taboo topics be shared.
- (e) Titles that name an outcome or identity, not a tactic.
- (f) Integrate monetisation into the advice rather than bolting on 60-s reads.
- (g) A catchphrase sign-off for ritual and community.

**7. What should not be combined.**
- **Don't copy the gorilla archetype.** Three channels already use it (A, B, Gorilla Bum). A fourth look-alike starts from zero distinctiveness.
- **Don't stack A's long sponsor reads on top of B's long emotional arcs.** Both cost retention, and viewers who come for a story will resent a 60-s ad read.
- **Don't mix A's search-literal titles with B's identity titles on the same channel.** They train different audiences and different traffic sources.
- **Don't carry over B's drug and porn shock humour if the target audience skews under 16.** Brand-safety and age-gating risk.

**8. Are they targeting similar audiences?** Largely yes. Both reach young men anxious about their bodies and discipline, and they share topics (testosterone, porn, height or skin). But they serve different *needs*. A is for "give me the gym answer, make it funny." B is for "explain why I keep failing, and give me a system." B's 2026 audience also skews more toward school and university.

**9. Is there a coherent opportunity for an original channel influenced by both?** Yes, at the level of **principles and format**, not look. The opportunity is a mascot-led, story-first, comedic explainer with an **ownable non-gorilla identity**. It would aim at the gaps both channels leave: accuracy, women, faith-framed self-control, social skills, creatine and supplement myths, native Shorts. See [10](10-opportunities.md).

**10. Adopt one model, combine, or go distinct?** **Adopt B's underlying model and borrow A's packaging discipline, under a distinct identity.**
- **From B:** story-first structure, identity titles, weekly cadence, and an owned funnel later. B's model is the one growing and the one with range.
- **From A:** single-pun thumbnails, search-literal titles for evergreen how-to topics, ruthless brevity on simple topics.
- **Avoid entirely:** the shared gorilla look, and topic overlap on generic testosterone and productivity.
