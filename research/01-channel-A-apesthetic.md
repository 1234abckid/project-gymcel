## 2. Positioning [Interpretation, built on Verified evidence]

- **Niche:** men's fitness and physique, the "natty/aesthetics" part of gym YouTube.
- **Sub-niche:** **hormone- and body-part explainers, delivered by a cartoon gorilla speaking caveman English.** Every title ends "…Explained By Ape" (22/22).
- **Category:** *educational-comedy*. The information is standard gym advice (compound lifts, calorie deficit, 7–9 h sleep, protein 1 g/lb). The product is the delivery.
- **Repeated subjects:** testosterone (6 videos), body-part growth (abs, biceps, shoulders, chest, V-taper), fat loss (6 videos), plus two sexual-health hits ("banana" = penis size; "gooning" = porn compulsion).
- **Central promise:** *"Bodybuilding as simple as possible."* The description boilerplate says: "Monkey hopes to make bodybuilding as simple as possible. Monkey want to make other monkeys looking big, strong and apesthetic."
- **Why choose A over Jeremy Ethier and other evidence-based fitness channels:** A is faster (most videos are 3–5 min) and funnier, it is safe to share in a group chat, and it turns embarrassing questions (low T, penis size, porn) into a joke you can admit to watching.
- **Consistency:** very high. One character, one background (white), one title template, one thumbnail grammar. The identity is recognisable with the name hidden.

## 5. Video construction (summary — autopsies in [05](05-storytelling-editing.md))

**Formula (observed in 10/10 transcripts):**
1. **Problem image (0–10 s):** a body insecurity shown on the gorilla ("No hard morning banana"; "Is this monkey thin? No. Is this monkey fat? No.").
2. *(Top performers only)* **a skit or a poem** before the promise.
3. **Promise:** "So today, ape teach you how to…" (arrives at 0:10–1:10).
4. **4–8 headed sections.** Each header is written on screen as a handwritten title with banana-sparkle icons ("Sleep Maxing", "Get Lean But Not Shredded"). Each section gives one mechanism and one instruction.
5. **Sponsor or merch insert** after section 2 or 3 (between 1:35 and 3:55).
6. **Recap + catchphrase sign-off** ("…monkey will look in the mirror and say, 'Wow…bananas'").

- **Pacing [Measured]:** median **172 WPM** (range 128–190). On the 2-second-sampled videos, the image changes on 64–73% of consecutive samples, which works out to an average hold of **≈ 2.7–3.1 s** per visual state.
- **No end screen talk, no subscribe begging** in any transcript.

**Reading [Interpretation]:** Taboo male-body topics outperform. Fat loss, the most-repeated 2026 pillar (5 uploads, Feb–Jul 2026), is the weakest. The newest uploads ("Hollow Cheeks", "Healthy Coconuts") show A moving into looksmaxxing and testicle health.

### Formats
1. **"How To [body goal] Explained By Ape"** (17/22): checklist explainer.
2. **"Why [problem]" / "Best [thing] for [goal]"**: variants of the same thing.
3. **Skit-led explainer** (Increase T, Banana): the cold-open scene is the differentiator.
4. **Poem-led explainer** (Stop Gooning): a one-off.

### Title system
- Average 44 characters. Title Case. No end punctuation. 17/22 start "How To". Numbers appear only for promises: "10x", "90 Days", "10%", "2 Minutes".
- The fixed suffix "Explained By Ape" works as a **brand marker and a search keyword at once**. Autocomplete for "explained by ape" returns *creatine / banana / abs / big banana explained by ape*.

## 9. Audience (detail in [06](06-audience-research.md))
**Hypothesis:** male, ~14–24, gym-curious beginners and "natty" lifters, fluent in meme slang (gooning, -maxxing, soy). Engagement is solid: a 5.9% median like rate. Recent breakouts show sharply lower like rates (0.76–2.6%), i.e. reach into colder, recommendation-fed audiences.

| The most legible thumbnails in the niche: white field, one huge brown silhouette, one pun | Topic well running dry within fitness. Fat loss repeated 6× with falling returns |
