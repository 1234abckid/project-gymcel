## 5. Comment-section questions to verify (when access is available)
1. **Who are they:** age cues ("I'm 15"), school vs work, region, faith references.
2. **Requests:** which topics are asked for repeatedly (B runs a public request board on Featurebase, which would be the richest single source).
3. **Running jokes:** "banana", "Stay Monke", "monke" catchphrases; whether viewers role-play in caveman English.
4. **Criticism:** repetition ("same advice"), accuracy pushback (testosterone numbers, ego depletion), ad fatigue (Cal AI), app scepticism (B), and confusion between the two channels.
5. **Cross-channel awareness:** do commenters notice that A and B look and sound alike?
6. **Outcome stories:** NoFap streaks, gym progress; signs of a loyalty / community loop.

## 6. Risks around this audience [Interpretation]
- **Minors plus adult humour:** both channels joke about porn, and B jokes about cocaine. The autocomplete evidence shows some of the topic demand comes from 12–15-year-olds. That raises brand-safety and possibly age-restriction risk, and it limits the advertiser pool.
- **Health claims:** a young audience is more likely to take literal advice (e.g. extreme cuts, "10x testosterone") and less able to tell contested science from settled science.
- **Looksmaxxing backlash:** 2026 coverage frames looksmaxxing as harmful to teen boys (see [07](07-niche-trend-report.md)). Channels close to that culture risk being lumped in with it, and there is room for a counter-positioned voice.
